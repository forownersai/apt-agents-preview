# VSL Script v2 — Apartment Agents (Dallas)
_Drafted 2026-08-04, after ingesting Hormozi's VSL framework (`skool-learn/hormozi-vsl/NOTES.md`). **v1 is kept at `vsl-script.md` for comparison.**_

**What changed from v1 and why:**
1. **Added a promise up front.** v1 opened cold on pain with no reason to keep watching.
2. **Added the FAQ block** — the six objections we already wrote for the Dallas page, in renters' own words. This was v1's biggest gap. Hormozi's point: every unanswered objection is a call you have to make later.
3. **First CTA moved early (≈4:00)**, second CTA at the end. Committed viewers leave at four minutes. Skeptical ones stay for the objections and convert at eight.
4. **Real proof stack** before the close instead of one review card.
5. **Testimonial tail** after the final CTA. Hormozi: people do watch them.

**Runtime:** ~8:00. **Evergreen:** nothing changes unless the reward changes.
**Only placeholder:** `[NAME]` in Beat 5.

**Why longer is right here even though John wanted tight:** the early CTA means we aren't asking anyone to sit through eight minutes. We're asking for four, and making the other four available to whoever still has doubts. A 2:50 video physically cannot carry six objections, and every objection we don't answer here, an agent answers on the phone later.

## BEAT 11 — THE FAQ BLOCK (4:35–7:05)
_Each question appears as an on-screen title card in the renter's words, then she answers. Shoot these as separate takes so any single answer can be re-cut without a reshoot._

**CARD: "Why would I use you? I can find an apartment myself."**
> You absolutely can, and plenty of people do. Here's the thing though. You'd be looking at the same apartments, at the same rent, because we don't mark anything up. The difference is you'd be doing all of it yourself, you'd only be seeing the ones that paid to be seen, and you'd be leaving up to two hundred and fifty dollars on the table.

**CARD: "Is this actually free? What's the catch?"**
> There isn't one, but there is a reason it works. You find a great apartment, the property gets a great resident, and they pay us for making the match. That's a cost they've already budgeted for, whether you come through us or not. It never costs you a dollar more than walking in on your own.

**CARD: "Aren't you just going to send me wherever pays you the most?"**
> Fair question, and I'll answer it straight instead of pretending we're neutral. We only work with communities that pay a referral fee, so that's the pool we're choosing from. But we don't get paid unless you actually sign a lease, so sending you somewhere wrong earns us nothing. And about half our business is repeat customers and referrals. One bad recommendation costs us far more than a bigger fee on one lease is worth.

**CARD: "I'm applying with my boyfriend / my roommate. Does it matter?"**
> Yes, and this is the one that costs people money. Only one locator gets paid per lease. So if you list us and the person you're applying with lists somebody else, or lists a website, or writes down a friend who works there, the community pays them and nobody gets the reward. Everyone on the application needs to put down Apartment Agents.

**CARD: "When do I actually get the money?"**
> After you move in, you submit a short claim form. We invoice the community, and once they pay us, we pay you. Zelle, Venmo, PayPal, or an Amazon gift card, whichever you want.

**CARD: "I've already been looking for weeks. Is it too late?"**
> Almost never. Most people have already started by the time they find us. The one thing that gets in the way is if you've already toured a place or already listed someone else as your referral source there. So just tell us where you've been, and we'll sort out quickly what still works.

## Guardrails (checked line by line against [[messaging-and-claims]] and [[reward-terms]])
- "up to two hundred and fifty," never a flat $250
- **match** it, not beat it
- no price-verification claim, no "hand-curated," no tour booking, **no mention of advancing the rebate**
- FAQ #5 describes the sequence without naming a timeframe, since 4–12 weeks is a terms figure and we flex on it
- movers: no cut, quotes come to us, they never get your contact info
- utilities: service described, independence never claimed (we do earn commission there)
- no claim that the only apartments you see are ones that paid, since free listings exist
- nothing about communities holding cheap units off market, permanently off camera
- no em dashes, no exclamation marks, no monthly or per-agent volume figures
