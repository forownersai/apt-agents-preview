_2026-08-04. **This is the one to shoot.** v1 (`vsl-script.md`, 2:50) and v2 (`vsl-script-v2.md`, 8:00) are kept for reference only._

**Runtime:** ~3:50 · **~570 spoken words** · **Evergreen** — changes only if the reward changes.
**Only placeholder:** `[NAME]` in Beat 6.

### Why this length
The decision we're asking for is "fill out a form for a free service," not "wire me five thousand dollars." Short runway. Hormozi's 30–45 minute VSLs exist to filter prospects before a closer spends an hour on them, and we don't want to filter — the bottleneck is speed-to-lead and follow-up, not lead quality. Add 95% mobile traffic and long-form on a phone is a drop-off machine.

### What moved out of the video, and where it went
| Content | New home | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 4 of the 6 objections (find it myself · roommate/authority · when do I get paid · already started looking) | **Accordion text below the video**, same page. Already written, already live on the Dallas page. | People with doubts scan text far faster than they sit through video. Same Hormozi principle, better format. |
| Guest card + roommate reward mechanics | **Post-signup onboarding** | It protects commission, but only matters once they're a customer. Teaching someone how they might forfeit a reward before they've asked for one is friction on the wrong axis. |
| Long testimonial tail | **Reviews section on the page** | Keep it, don't make it runtime. |

The two objections that stayed are the two that actually block a signup. Money is off the table because the service is free, so **trust is the only objection left.**

### Measure before extending
Host where drop-off is visible. Still watching at 90% when it ends → earn the right to extend. Gone at 60 seconds → the FAQ block was never the issue.

## BEAT 1 — HOOK (0:00–0:12)
_On camera. Cold. No overlay._

> Have you ever noticed that when you're searching on the big apartment websites, apartments pop up that aren't even in the area you were looking? And the same few places keep showing up no matter what you change?

## BEAT 2 — PROMISE (0:12–0:28)

> There's a reason for that, and I'm going to show it to you using those websites' own sales pages. Then I'll show you how to get the apartment you actually want, and get up to two hundred and fifty dollars back for leasing it.

## BEAT 3 — PAIN (0:28–0:52)

> Right now you're probably twenty tabs deep. You've called three places and left messages at all three. Two of the listings you liked are already gone, and one was never really available at that price.
>
> And you still don't know if any of them are any good.

## BEAT 4 — PROOF / THE TWIST (0:52–1:38)
_Calm delivery. The graphics do the accusing._

> Those websites don't work for you. You've never paid them a dollar. The apartments pay them, and what they're paying for is position.

**OVERLAY →** `apartments-com-hero.png` (hold 3s, URL visible)

> This is apartments.com's own sales page. Not my words. Theirs.

**OVERLAY →** `apartments-com-tiers-row.png` (hold 4s, push in)

> Diamond. Platinum. Gold. Three hundred percent more exposure.

**OVERLAY →** `apartments-com-tiers-full.png`, punch in on **"Placement above Gold listings"** (hold 3s)

> Placement above Gold listings. That's a paid ladder, and you're the product being sold. The apartments that don't buy in are still out there. You just never see them.

## BEAT 5 — THE HIDDEN COST (1:38–2:05)

> And here's the part that actually costs you. A listing tells you the square footage and whether they take dogs. It won't tell you the walls are thin, or that maintenance takes two weeks, or that management just changed hands, or that there's a bar behind the building that goes until two.
>
> You find that out after you've signed a twelve month lease.

## BEAT 6 — WHO + THE PLAN (2:05–2:48)
**OVERLAY →** Apartment List Builder screenshot, results view. Hold through the beat.

> I'm [NAME] with Apartment Agents. Licensed Texas brokerage, nothing but apartment rentals since 2003.
>
> You tell us where you want to be, your budget, your move-in date, and what matters most to you. Your licensed apartment expert scours the market for the best-priced floor plans and move-in specials, then sends you the ones that fit, with our top picks up front.
>
> And we keep our own database on the places we send people. Which management companies actually answer. Which buildings get noise complaints. No listing site tracks that.

## BEAT 7 — THE STACK (2:48–3:06)

> You tour whatever you want, whenever suits you. No agent tagging along. Tell us which movers you want quotes from and they come to us, so the movers never get your phone number. And we'll get your utilities and internet set up before you move.

## BEAT 8 — THE MONEY (3:06–3:25)
**OVERLAY →** "Up to $250 cash back" title card

> Then when you lease, you get up to two hundred and fifty dollars cash back. Highest in Dallas, and if you find a locator offering more on the same apartment, we'll match it. Paid straight to you.

## BEAT 9 — THE TWO OBJECTIONS (3:25–3:52)
_No title cards. Let her run it as one thought so it stays conversational and fast._

> Two things people always ask me. First, what's the catch. There isn't one. The property pays us for sending them a great resident, and that's budgeted whether you come through us or not. It never costs you a dollar more than walking in alone.
>
> Second, am I just going to send you wherever pays me the most. We only work with communities that pay, so that's the pool. But we don't get paid unless you actually sign, and about half our business is repeat and referral. One bad recommendation costs us a lot more than one bigger fee is worth.

## BEAT 10 — CTA (3:52–4:02)

> Fill out the form below. It costs you nothing, and you'll hear from a licensed Dallas expert who does this all day.

## Guardrails (checked against [[messaging-and-claims]] and [[reward-terms]])
- "up to two hundred and fifty," never a flat $250
- **match** it, not beat it
- no price-verification claim, no "hand-curated," no tour booking, **no mention of advancing the rebate**
- no payment timeframe named
- movers: no cut taken, quotes come to us, they never get contact info
- utilities: service described, independence never claimed (we do earn commission there)
- no claim that the only apartments you see are ones that paid — free listings exist
- nothing about communities holding cheap units off market, permanently off camera
- no em dashes, no exclamation marks, no monthly or per-agent volume figures
